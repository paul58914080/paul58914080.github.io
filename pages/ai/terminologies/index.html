    Terminologies¶

    This section provides definitions and explanations of key terms and concepts related to Artificial Intelligence (AI). Understanding these terminologies is essential for anyone looking to delve into the field of AI, whether you're a beginner or an experienced practitioner.

    Artificial Intelligence (AI)¶

    • Definition: The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems. These processes include learning (the acquisition of information and rules for using the information), reasoning (using rules to reach approximate or definite conclusions), and self-correction.
    • Example: Virtual assistants like Siri and Alexa, recommendation systems on Netflix and Amazon.
    Types of AI
    • Generative AI: AI systems that can create new content, such as text, images, or music, based on learned patterns from existing data (e.g., GPT-3, DALL-E).
    • Narrow AI: Designed for a specific task (e.g., facial recognition).
    • General AI: Aims to perform any intellectual task that a human can do (still theoretical).
    • Superintelligent AI: Surpasses human intelligence (still theoretical).
    • Reactive Machines: Basic AI systems that can only react to current situations (e.g., IBM's Deep Blue).
    • Limited Memory: AI systems that can use past experiences to inform future decisions (e.g self-driving cars).
    • Theory of Mind: AI systems that can understand human emotions and social interactions (still in development).
    • Self-aware AI: AI systems that possess self-awareness and consciousness (still theoretical).
    • Hybrid AI: Combines multiple AI techniques to achieve better performance (e.g., combining machine learning and rule-based systems).
    • Explainable AI (XAI): AI systems designed to provide transparent and understandable explanations for their decisions and actions.
    • Federated Learning: A machine learning technique that enables training models across multiple decentralized devices or servers while keeping data localized, enhancing privacy and security.
    • Edge AI: AI processing that occurs on local devices (like smartphones or IoT devices) rather than relying on cloud computing, reducing latency and improving privacy.
    • Reinforcement Learning: A type of machine learning where an agent learns to make decisions by taking actions in an environment to maximize cumulative rewards.
    • Transfer Learning: A machine learning technique where a pre-trained model is adapted to a new but related task, reducing the need for large amounts of training data.
    • Multimodal AI: AI systems that can process and integrate information from multiple modalities, such as text, images, and audio, to enhance understanding and performance.
    • Neuro-symbolic AI: An approach that combines neural networks with symbolic reasoning to leverage the strengths of both methods for improved AI capabilities.

    Generative AI¶

    • Definition: A subset of AI that focuses on creating new content, such as text, images, music, or even code, by learning patterns from existing data.
    • Example: OpenAI's GPT-3 for text generation, DALL-E for image generation.

    AI Agents¶

    • Definition: Autonomous entities that perceive their environment, make decisions, and take actions to achieve specific goals.
    • Example: Chatbots, virtual assistants, and robotic process automation (RPA) bots

    Agentic AI¶

    • Definition: AI systems that exhibit agency, meaning they can make decisions and take actions independently to achieve specific objectives.
    • Example: Autonomous vehicles that navigate and make driving decisions without human intervention.

    Machine Learning (ML)¶

    • Definition: A subset of AI that enables systems to learn from data and improve their performance over time without being explicitly programmed.
    • Example: Email spam filters, fraud detection systems.

    Deep Learning¶

    • Definition: A subset of machine learning that uses neural networks with multiple layers (deep neural networks) to model complex patterns in data.
    • Example: Image and speech recognition systems.

    Natural Language Processing (NLP)¶

    • Definition: A branch of AI that focuses on the interaction between computers and human language, enabling machines to understand, interpret, and generate human language.
    • Example: Language translation services, sentiment analysis tools.

    Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)¶

    • Definition: A technique that combines retrieval-based methods with generative models to enhance the quality and relevance of generated content by incorporating external knowledge.
    • Example: Using a knowledge base to provide context for a language model's responses.

    Vector Database¶

    • Definition: A specialized database designed to store and manage high-dimensional vector representations of data, often used in AI applications for tasks like similarity search and recommendation systems.
    • Example: Pinecone, FAISS.

    Large Language Models (LLMs)¶

    • Definition: Advanced AI models trained on vast amounts of text data to understand and generate human-like language. They can perform a wide range of language-related tasks.
    • Example: OpenAI's GPT-3, Google's BERT.

    Foundation Models¶

    • Definition: Large-scale AI models that serve as a base for various downstream tasks. They are pre-trained on extensive datasets and can be fine-tuned for specific applications.
    • Example: GPT-3, BERT, CLIP.

    Communication¶

    Query¶

    The immediate question or instruction you give to the AI. Example in AI: Query: “Write me a short story about a dragon.” The AI will try to answer that specific request.

    Context¶

    The surrounding information or memory that helps the AI interpret your query correctly. Example in AI: Previous chats: “I like stories in the style of Harry Potter.” Query: “Write me a short story about a dragon.” With context, AI may write it in a Harry Potter–like style, not just any dragon story.

    Prompt¶

    The full set of instructions (query + optional context + formatting/style hints) you provide to guide the AI’s output. Example in AI: Prompt: “Write a 200-word story about a dragon in the style of Harry Potter. Use humor, and make sure the dragon has a quirky habit like knitting sweaters.” That’s a crafted, detailed instruction = a prompt.
